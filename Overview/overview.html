Context

Liver cancer is among the most aggressive and lethal diseases in oncology, characterised by its late diagnosis and the limited number of therapeutic options available at advanced stages. Despite advances in medicine, it remains one of the leading causes of mortality worldwide, affecting millions of individuals and their families. Confronting this disease poses not only a medical challenge but also has a profound emotional and psychological impact on both patients and their loved ones.

Motivation

The impetus for undertaking this study stems from a personal experience that profoundly shaped my life. I lost my mother after years of battling liver cancer, a fight she faced with courage and determination. Her strength and resilience were a constant source of inspiration to me, as was her unwavering support and encouragement to continue my education and pursue my goals and aspirations. Her passing left an immense void, but also ignited in me a deep need to better understand this illness and the process she underwent. I felt that through knowledge, I could find a way to honour her memory and, perhaps, contribute to ensuring that others do not have to endure the same suffering.

I have always believed in the importance of engaging in projects that truly resonate with me—endeavours to which I can contribute as much as I gain from them. This study reflects that philosophy: more than an academic undertaking, it represents a commitment to medical research, to the exploration of emerging technologies, and to the aspiration of improving the early detection of liver cancer. Throughout this process, I have sought to combine my background in data science with the potential of Artificial Intelligence to address a real-world issue with tangible medical applications.

Objetive

This study aims to analyse and compare various Artificial Intelligence approaches—specifically Machine Learning and Deep Learning—for the prediction of liver cancer. Through a rigorous process of collecting, cleaning, and analysing clinical data, the objective has been to develop models capable of identifying hidden patterns and enhancing the accuracy of disease detection. The ultimate goal is not only to determine which model yields the best results, but also to assess its feasibility in a real clinical context, taking into account factors such as interpretability, ease of implementation, and practical utility for healthcare professionals.

Conclusions

This has been not only a technical challenge, but also a personal journey of learning and reflection. Through it, I have been able to delve deeper into the impact of liver cancer and the challenges faced by both clinicians and patients in its diagnosis and treatment. While technology alone cannot solve all the problems in medicine, it can serve as a powerful ally in early detection and clinical decision-making. With this work, I hope to make a modest contribution to the fight against this disease and, in some way, pay tribute to the person who inspired me never to stop learning and to always seek ways to help others.
